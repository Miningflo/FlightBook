FlightBook privacy policy

Last updated: 23 November 2026

FlightBook is a personal pilot logbook that runs entirely in your browser. It has no server of its own: the developer does not receive, store or see your logbook or any other data about you.

In short. Your logbook never leaves your device unless you export it or connect your own Google Drive. Nothing is sent to the developer, ever. There is no analytics, no tracking and no advertising. The app contacts three outside services, all of them optional or on your explicit action, and each one only ever learns your IP address and (for photos) an aircraft registration — never a logbook entry.

Data stored on your device

Everything is stored locally in your browser (IndexedDB):

  • Your logbook: flights with dates, times, places, aircraft, flight and duty times and your free-text remarks; simulator sessions; previous-experience carry-in totals; aircraft; and any files you attach to a flight.
  • Your details as logbook holder: your name, your licence number and your address. These are optional and are used for the holder block the regulation expects at the front of a printed logbook (AMC1 FCL.050). They are printed into the PDF you generate.
  • People you log: the names of the pilots in command and instructors you fly with (see Other people in your logbook).
  • Settings and caches: app settings, the airport database if you import one, and cached aircraft photo metadata. On a read-only viewer device, the last Google Drive snapshot you loaded.

This data stays on your device until you delete it. Clearing the site's data in your browser, or uninstalling the app, deletes all of it. There is no copy anywhere else unless you made one.

This storage is strictly necessary for the app you asked to use — a logbook that works offline cannot work without it — so FlightBook shows no cookie banner. It sets no cookies at all, and stores nothing for analytics, profiling or advertising.

Other people in your logbook

A logbook necessarily records other people: the name of the pilot in command, of an instructor, or whatever you write in the remarks. That is personal data about them, and it sits on your device under your control, not the developer's.

If you keep the logbook purely for yourself, EU data-protection law generally treats this as a personal or household activity and does not impose further duties on you (Article 2(2)(c) GDPR). If you keep it in a professional capacity — as an instructor, examiner, or because an employer or operator requires it — that exemption may not apply and you may have your own obligations towards the people you record. Keeping the entries accurate, writing no more in the remarks than you need, and not passing exports around are sensible precautions either way.

Google user data

If you choose to connect Google Drive, FlightBook asks for one permission: https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file. This only gives access to files that FlightBook itself created in your Drive (the "Logbook backups" folder and its contents), not to any other file in your Drive. FlightBook does not request your e-mail address, profile, contacts or any other Google data.

  • What is accessed: the backup snapshots and the master.json file that FlightBook writes.
  • How it is used: only to back up your logbook and to restore or view it on your own devices.
  • Where it goes: directly between your browser and Google. Nothing is sent to the developer or to any other party.
  • Sharing: Google user data is not sold, shared, transferred or used for advertising, and it is not used to train AI or machine-learning models.
  • Retention and deletion: the files stay in your Google Drive under your control. You can delete them in Drive at any time. "Disconnect Google Drive" in FlightBook's settings revokes access; you can also remove FlightBook at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

FlightBook's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Alerts for currency and class ratings are generated on your device by the app itself. They do not use a push service: nothing about them is sent anywhere.

Hosting and international transfers

The app is served by GitHub Pages, which may log technical request data under GitHub's privacy statement. FlightBook uses no analytics, cookies for tracking, or advertising.

GitHub and Google are established in the United States, so contacting them involves a transfer of the technical data described above outside the European Economic Area. Both rely on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses; their own privacy policies, linked above, set out the details. Your logbook itself is only transferred if you choose to connect Google Drive.

Legal basis

For the limited processing that happens when you load the app (your browser's request reaching the host), the basis is the legitimate interest in delivering the page you asked for. Everything beyond that — connecting Google Drive, looking up aircraft photos, importing the airport database, enabling notifications — happens only on a choice you make and can withdraw at any time, in the app's settings or your browser's. Your logbook content is not processed by the developer on any basis, because it is never received.

Your rights

Because the data is on your device and the developer never receives it, you exercise most data-protection rights directly in the app rather than by asking anyone:

  • Access and rectification: every record is visible and editable in the app.
  • Erasure: delete individual records in the app, or clear the site's data in your browser to remove everything. Backups in your Google Drive are deleted in Drive.
  • Portability: Settings → Export, import & backup produces your complete data as JSON, as CSV, or as a printable PDF logbook. These files contain personal data — keep them somewhere safe.
  • Objection and restriction: every optional feature above can be switched off.

There is no automated decision-making or profiling. The currency and alert calculations are arithmetic on your own entries, shown to you with the regulation they come from.

If you believe your data has been handled unlawfully, you can complain to your national data protection authority. In Belgium this is the Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit / Autorité de protection des données; elsewhere in the EEA, the authority for your country.

Changes to this policy

If the app starts contacting a new service or asking for a new permission, this page is updated before that change ships, and the date at the top changes with it. Past versions are visible in the project's git history.

Contact

FlightBook is an open-source project maintained by the owner of github.com/Miningflo/FlightBook. For any question about this policy or about your data, open an issue there.
